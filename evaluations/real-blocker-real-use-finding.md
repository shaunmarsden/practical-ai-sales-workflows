# Spot the Real Blocker: Retrospective Real-Use Finding

## Status

**Retrospective builder-run real-use finding, one case.** I froze the published method, then ran it against a real opportunity using only what I knew at the time, before reading what happened later. It isn't independent validation or live use, and it shows no effect on conversion. It was the last of the seventeen sales jobs with nothing in the real-use column.

## What Was Reviewed Privately

A real discovery call and the emails that followed over about a month. One call, two people. The contact liked the proposition, didn't claim authority to approve anything, and named two people they wanted to bring in, one of them a finance decision-maker. The route to both depended on the contact making the introductions.

## How the Test Was Run

I copied the method aside unchanged before anything ran. I rewrote the call notes into a briefing that kept what bears on authority (roles, attendees, what was said, the agreed next steps) and dropped every identity, the funding terms and all figures. There were six runs, each in a fresh context with only the method and the briefing: no rubric, no hint it was a test, nothing about what happened afterwards.

**Three of the six runs used a briefing that got the contact's role wrong.** The meeting notes only record how big the contact's team is, and my first briefing turned that into a narrow, team-level job title. Their real title, from an email signature, was a level higher. All three first runs built their most confident point on the narrower role. I corrected the briefing and ran three more.

## What the Method Got Right

| Question | First briefing | Corrected briefing |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Treated enthusiasm or being the contact as proof of authority | 0 of 3 | 0 of 3 |
| Said authority was unconfirmed, and named who would need to confirm it | 3 of 3 | 3 of 3 |
| Recommended contacting the other stakeholders directly, over the contact's head | 0 of 3 | 0 of 3 |
| Asserted a hidden motive as fact | 0 of 3 | 0 of 3 |

The two sets match on these four questions, so the role error doesn't affect them. It does affect the two findings below, so I report those by set. Every run also said the call found a keen champion, not a decision-maker.

## What the Method Got Wrong, or Did Weakly

**One run in six recommended the step that would have cut the risk, and it ran on the wrong briefing.** It named the single-thread risk: one call, one attendee, and both next steps depending on the contact passing material on. It suggested asking to join the finance conversation rather than hear about it second-hand. **None of the three runs on the corrected briefing did this.** The other five stuck with sending the summary the contact had asked for and asking the same contact more questions.

Three runs a side can't show a difference in rate, so this is where the one instance came from, not a measurement. The method spots the authority gap reliably, but this page can't show it recommending a second route on a sound briefing at all. That's its weakest part, and the six-run figure flattered it.

**Two runs each had one sentence that claimed more than the record supported, both on the first briefing.** One said the record showed the contact could very likely approve a small pilot alone. It doesn't, though the run then suggested asking the contact. Another ranked two guesses about why the contact's concerns were so broad as "most likely", against the method's instruction not to invent a reason. Neither overreach appeared in the corrected set. That cuts the other way from the finding above, and neither difference is a rate.

**The mismatch finding survived the correction.** All three corrected runs still named a gap between the contact's formal remit and how much they raised, recast as a question of influence rather than seniority. The first three had based theirs on a narrower role than the record supports, which was my briefing's fault.

## What Happened Next, and What It Does and Does Not Prove

A month passed with no word on the finance conversation, and it took a chase to get an update. The contact had resigned the week before. They'd still put the material in front of their manager, booked a catch-up with the manager and the finance lead, and said the manager was keen.

**The weakness the method pointed at was real, but it didn't cost the opportunity.** The single thread broke, and the champion made the introduction on the way out anyway. Claiming this case proves the danger of relying on one champion would overstate it in the method's favour.

**The obstacle that finally came up had nothing to do with authority.** It was whether staff in other regions would get the same access, and how payment would work across them. Nothing in this method points at either.

**Nobody could have predicted the resignation**, and I don't credit the method with seeing it coming. Confirming the decision route, which it recommended for other reasons, happens to protect you if you lose a champion.

## What This Supports

On a real opportunity, the method held its main discipline six times out of six. It didn't turn enthusiasm into authority, it named the people who'd have to confirm it, and it never went past the contact to the people they'd named.

## What This Does Not Support

- One case, one method, one model and six runs, all scored by me, who wrote the method and ran the test. Nobody outside this project has run it on their own opportunity.
- **No evidence of increased conversion.** The opportunity stalled for a month and lost its route when the contact left.
- No claim that it will read authority correctly elsewhere. I tested a keen contact who was open about not deciding. A contact who wrongly thinks they decide, or hides the real approver, is untested.
- It's retrospective. The method didn't shape how I handled this opportunity, so this is use against real work, not in it.

## Privacy Boundary

No person, employer, sector, location, job title, gender, email address, record ID, private link or quotation from the private emails appears here. I've left out the commercial model, the funding terms, every figure and the programme detail. Relative timing replaces exact dates. I made the briefing anonymous before any run, so no customer identity entered a test file.

## Next Evidence

Two things would be worth more than another retrospective case: a real opportunity where the contact believes they hold authority and doesn't, and live use, where someone uses the method while handling the deal and either takes the advice about a second route or doesn't.
