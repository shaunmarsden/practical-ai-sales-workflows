# Sales Copilot Live-Run Finding

## Status

Internal live-run evidence for the public Sales Copilot composition method. I handled one real sales request using approved connected sources and the public rules for combining workflows. It prepared a useful next action without changing anything outside. I built the method and ran it myself, so this isn't independent validation or proof that it's reliable.

## Live Request

I used the copilot's "What should I do next?" mode: a single question, with no opportunity or company named. The calendar had a fixed external meeting coming up soon. The method put that meeting ahead of a wider pipeline search, as the decision rule in the [approval-gated template](../templates/approval-gated-sales-copilot-template.md) says it should.

## Sources Used

It only pulled evidence that could change the decision: the calendar, the relevant CRM records, relevant email, approved meeting-note history, and the instructions for the installed pre-call workflow. It didn't scan the wider pipeline.

There wasn't much evidence. It could find the organisation and the CRM opportunity, but the attendee's identity didn't fully match across the connected records. It found no useful earlier email thread, and there was no approved meeting-note history. There wasn't enough to infer pain, authority, budget, urgency, programme fit or a detailed purpose for the meeting. It confirmed the pre-call workflow was installed and current before using it.

## Decision Prepared

Focus on the meeting coming up, not the wider pipeline. The evidence didn't support pain, authority, budget, urgency, programme fit or a detailed purpose, so it prepared a discovery-first opening. That meant confirming who was attending and asking the meeting's purpose directly, rather than inventing personal context the evidence didn't support. Nothing was sent, booked, changed or written back to any outside system.

## What Worked

It put the fixed meeting first and kept its search narrow. It checked the pre-call workflow before relying on it. It said plainly that there was no useful email thread or meeting history, instead of padding the answer with unrelated activity. It flagged that the attendee records didn't fully match, instead of quietly treating two records as the same person. It left authority, budget, urgency, programme fit and motivation as unknown rather than guessing from thin evidence. It still prepared a useful next step, and it kept every outside action waiting for approval.

## Human Check

Before the meeting, the salesperson still had to confirm the attendee's identity and find out the meeting's real purpose in the room. The connected records couldn't settle who the attendee was, and the copilot rightly declined to guess.

## What This Supports

- The public composition method can put a fixed commitment ahead of a broad pipeline search.
- It can keep its search narrow rather than searching every connected system by default.
- It can check a specialist workflow before relying on it.
- It can say when connected sources hold little or no useful context, instead of making up a fuller-looking answer.
- It can flag that two records may or may not be the same person, rather than assuming they are.
- It can leave authority, budget, urgency, fit and motivation unknown rather than guessing them to look complete.
- It can prepare a useful next action without changing anything outside.
- It kept outside actions waiting for approval throughout this run.

## What This Does Not Support

- That the Sales Copilot is reliable across all its command modes.
- That this was an independent test. I built the method and ran it.
- Any measured time saving.
- Any improved conversion, revenue or productivity.
- That every connected record was complete. Several were thin or missing.
- That the attendee question was settled. It was flagged, not resolved.
- That the public template ran unchanged as a standalone production agent. This is one use of the method, not a deployed system running unattended.
- That any outside action was taken. Nothing was sent, booked, changed or written back to a connected system.
- That the private records behind this run can be published. They can't.

This is one live use of the public composition method, run by me.

## Privacy Boundary

This page contains no real person's name, employer or prospect name, email address, calendar link, meeting link, CRM URL or record ID, booking link, exact meeting time or title, job title, private programme detail or exact private message, and none should be inferred from it. I haven't published any redacted copy of the real calendar entry, CRM record or emails. I describe the identity mismatch only as a limit I saw, not as a description of who was involved.

The composition method already requires checking attendees and CRM records before preparing for a meeting. This run handled the mismatch the way the method asks, so I record it as a real-world limit worth knowing, not as a sign that an instruction needs to change.
