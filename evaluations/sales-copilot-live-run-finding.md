# Sales Copilot Live-Run Finding

## Status

Internal live-run evidence for the public Sales Copilot composition method. One real sales request was handled using approved connected sources and the public orchestration rules. The run produced a useful prepared next action without making an external change. This is builder-run evidence, not independent validation or proof of reliability.

## Live Request

The request was the copilot's "What should I do next?" mode: a single question with no named opportunity or company attached. An imminent fixed external meeting existed on the calendar. The method prioritised that fixed commitment over widening into a broader pipeline search, consistent with the decision standard in the [approval-gated template](../templates/approval-gated-sales-copilot-template.md).

## Sources Used

Only evidence that could materially change the decision was retrieved: the calendar, the relevant CRM records, relevant email, approved meeting-note history, and the installed pre-call specialist workflow's instructions. No wider pipeline scan was run.

The live evidence was sparse. The relevant organisation and CRM opportunity could be resolved, but attendee identity was not completely consistent across the connected records. No useful prior email thread was found, and no prior approved meeting-note history existed. There was not enough evidence to infer pain, authority, budget, urgency, programme fit, or a detailed meeting objective. The relevant pre-call specialist route was confirmed installed and current before being used.

## Decision Prepared

Focus on the imminent meeting rather than the wider pipeline. Because the evidence did not support pain, authority, budget, urgency, programme fit, or a detailed objective, the prepared approach was a discovery-first opening: confirm who is actually attending and establish the meeting's purpose directly, rather than inventing personalised context the evidence did not support. Nothing was sent, booked, changed, or written back to any external system.

## What Worked

- Prioritised a fixed external commitment over a broad pipeline search.
- Kept retrieval narrow: only sources that could actually change the decision were used.
- Verified the relevant pre-call specialist route was installed and current before relying on it.
- Reported plainly that no useful prior email thread and no prior approved meeting-note history existed, rather than padding the result with unrelated activity.
- Surfaced that attendee identity was not completely consistent across connected records, rather than silently treating two records as definitely the same person.
- Kept authority, budget, urgency, programme fit and motivation explicitly unknown rather than inferring them from thin evidence.
- Prepared a genuinely useful next action, a discovery-first opening, without making any external change.
- Kept every external action approval-gated throughout.

## Human Check

Before the meeting, the salesperson still needed to confirm the attendee's identity directly and establish the actual meeting purpose in the room. The connected records did not resolve the identity question on their own, and the copilot correctly declined to guess rather than treating the ambiguous records as settled.

## What This Supports

- The public composition method can prioritise a fixed commitment over a broad pipeline search.
- It can keep retrieval narrow rather than defaulting to a wide search across every connected system.
- It can verify a specialist route before relying on it.
- It can report when connected sources contain little or no useful context, instead of manufacturing a fuller-looking answer.
- It can surface an identity ambiguity rather than silently treating two records as definitely the same person.
- It can preserve unknown authority, budget, urgency, fit and motivation rather than inferring them to look more complete.
- It can prepare a useful next action without making an external change.
- It kept external actions approval-gated throughout this run.

## What This Does Not Support

- That the Sales Copilot is reliable across all of its command modes.
- That this was an independent test; it was run by the same person who built the method.
- Any measured time saving.
- Any improved conversion, revenue or productivity.
- That every connected record was complete; several were sparse or absent.
- That the attendee ambiguity was actually resolved; it was surfaced, not settled.
- That the public template was installed as a standalone production agent unchanged; this describes one application of the method, not a deployed, unattended system.
- That any external action was taken. Nothing was sent, booked, changed, or written back to a connected system.
- That the private source records behind this run can be published. They cannot.

This is one builder-run live application of the public composition method.

## Privacy Boundary

No real person's name, employer or prospect name, email address, calendar link, meeting link, CRM URL or record identifier, booking link, exact meeting time, exact meeting title, job title, private programme detail, or exact private message is included here, and none should be inferred from what is written. No redacted copy of the real calendar entry, CRM record, or correspondence is published. The identity ambiguity described above is reported only as an observed limitation, not as a description of who was actually involved.

The existing composition method already requires attendee and CRM-record verification before a meeting is prepared. This run's ambiguity was handled the way the method already asks for, so it is recorded as a real-world limitation worth knowing about, not as evidence that an instruction needs to change.
