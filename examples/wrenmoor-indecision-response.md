# Wrenmoor Buyer Indecision Response

> This is a worked response created from the [fictional Wrenmoor scenario](wrenmoor-indecision-input.md). Nothing has been sent, and no discount, guarantee, pilot extension or commitment has been offered or authorised.

## What This Actually Is

Indecision, not a sequence of real blockers. Farrah is positive, holds a resolved budget, has a settled seat count, and has no outstanding concern about price, product or integration. What identifies this as indecision is not any single reason, each one was individually legitimate while open, but the pattern across all three: every stated dependency has been cleared, and each clearing has produced a new reason rather than a decision. That is the signature of decision risk, the fear of committing, wearing the shape of due diligence.

## What It Is Not

- **Not a real sequential dependency.** A genuine chain of blockers ends in a decision once the last one clears. Here, clearing sign-off produced the headcount question, and clearing the headcount question produced the offsite. The chain has no visible end.
- **Not an objection.** Nothing about price, the product, data handling or the vendor has been contested at any point.
- **Not a stakeholder-approval gate, not any more.** The one real approval gate, her VP's budget sign-off, has already cleared. What remains is Farrah's own readiness, not someone else's authority.
- **Not gone quiet.** She replies promptly, warmly, and volunteers information without being asked.
- **Not a disqualification.** Fit, value and budget are all established and confirmed.
- **Not simply a timing issue with a real reason**, even though the offsite looks like one. A single dated event would be a legitimate reason to wait. A third dated event, arriving immediately after the second reason cleared with no commitment in between, is the pattern, not the exception to it.

## Why the Obvious Moves Would Backfire

Treating the offsite as the final real gate and just waiting would repeat what has already happened twice: a cleared dependency followed by a new one, with the underlying hesitation never actually named. Pushing to skip or shorten the offsite, or adding urgency or a discount to force a decision before it, risks the same backfire as with any fearful buyer: it reads as pressure to commit before she is ready, which tends to deepen the hesitation rather than resolve it. The offsite itself is not the problem to solve; the pattern underneath it is.

## The Approach: Reduce the Risk of Deciding, Without Contesting the Offsite

- **Name the pattern gently, not the offsite.** Do not challenge whether the offsite is a legitimate reason to wait, it likely is, for walking her team through the decision properly. Instead, gently note the pattern: three separate things have each been resolved, and each time a new one has taken its place, and ask if that is really about the offsite or about something else not yet said.
- **Make a clear recommendation for after the offsite, not before it.** Do not ask her to decide early or shorten her plan. Ask her to agree what "right after" the offsite actually means, so the fourth resolved item does not quietly produce a fifth.
- **Keep her own evidence in front of her.** Her team's own reported time saving is the strongest counterweight to any unspoken fear of getting the call wrong, because it is her data, not a vendor claim.
- **Offer a smaller, reversible first step only if one genuinely exists.** If a phased or partial rollout starting with the team that felt the benefit most is actually available, it can be offered as an option to discuss after the offsite. If no such term exists, it must not be invented here.

## Draft Reply

> Hi Farrah,
>
> Not dragging your feet at all, and I appreciate you being upfront about the offsite. That makes sense as a reason to want the team properly briefed before you're locked in.
>
> One thing I want to name gently, not to push on the offsite itself: budget, then seats, have each come together exactly as you said they would, and each time there's been a new piece to settle before moving. That's not a criticism, it might just be how thorough you want to be here, but I want to check: is the offsite genuinely the last piece, or is there something else about this that's still not settled for you?
>
> Either way, I'm happy to hold until after the offsite. Could we agree roughly what "right after" looks like on your side, a specific week rather than "sometime after", so we've got something concrete to work back from? And in the meantime, it's worth keeping your team's own numbers in front of you, cutting that reporting task from a day to under an hour is real evidence, not anything from me.
>
> Shaun

## Pipeline Decision

Keep active, and do not accept the offsite as the fourth unresolved gate without checking. The next step is the gentle pattern-naming question plus a request to pin down what "right after" the offsite actually means, so a fourth open-ended reason cannot quietly appear once the offsite passes. If Farrah's next reply produces a new reason after the offsite has been and gone, that would confirm the indecision read strongly enough to raise it directly rather than gently, and to consider whether something unspoken, not yet on record, is the real driver.

## Notes for Human Review

- No discount, guarantee, urgency, or shortened offsite has been suggested. The offsite itself is treated as legitimate throughout; only the pattern around it is questioned.
- No specific date has been invented for "right after" the offsite. The reply asks Farrah to name one rather than assuming or proposing a date she has not given.
- The "phased or partial rollout" option is deliberately left conditional in the method above and not included in the draft reply itself, since the scenario does not establish that term as genuinely available. If it is available, Shaun would need to add it explicitly before sending.
- This diagnosis rests on a pattern across three resolved reasons, not on any one of them. If a fourth reason appears after the offsite and it also resolves without a decision, that would strengthen this read further; if instead a specific, contestable concern finally surfaces, this should be re-diagnosed as an objection at that point, not forced to remain indecision.
