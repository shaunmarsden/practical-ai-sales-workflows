# Fictional Objection Pattern Log: Fourth Test

> This log is entirely fictional, created to test whether the Review Objection Patterns skill can find a genuine pattern hiding behind two opposite-looking behaviours, rather than only behind similar wording. The first three tests each involve similar words masking different drivers, or a driver spanning several similarly-worded entries. This one inverts that: two entries that look nothing alike on the surface, one an abrupt call termination, one a calm, professional request, share the same underlying driver. The log also includes an entry with a stated alternative reason for the same surface trigger, and an entry with no stated reason at all. Every company, person and outcome is invented.

## Deliberate Test Points

- Entries 1 and 2 are the genuine pattern this log is built to test, and they look nothing alike. Fenmore Cross ends the call abruptly out of alarm; Bellcross Analytics calmly asks for an NDA and a reference. Both are the same underlying need, confirming the programme is genuine and does what it claims, before proceeding, expressed at opposite ends of the same spectrum: panic versus professional diligence. Grouping them requires reading past the surface behaviour to the shared driver, the reverse of the earlier tests in this series, which grouped similar wording with different drivers.
- This pattern rests on exactly two distinct deals, the minimum the skill's own rules treat as a genuine candidate rather than an isolated signal. Confidence should reflect that thin a sample honestly, not be inflated to match the stronger, larger patterns found in earlier tests.
- Entry 4 shares Entry 1's exact surface trigger, being asked for a National Insurance number and date of birth, but Oswin Vantage Group's own later message explicitly states an unrelated reason for the silence. This should not be folded into the legitimacy pattern just because the initial trigger matches; the applicant's own words rule it out directly.
- Entry 5 shares Entry 2's surface request, wanting to speak to someone who has done the programme, but the underlying need is different: Redgate had already independently confirmed the provider's legitimacy and said so, so their request is about outcome proof, not trust in the scheme itself. Grouping Entry 5 with Entry 2 would be a surface-wording error, the same trap the second and third tests in this series were built to catch, applied here to a request rather than an objection.
- Entry 6 is a narrow, self-contained factual question, included as a plain distractor unrelated to either pattern.
- Entry 7 has no stated reason at all, and nothing earlier in the exchange points to one. This should be reported as an entry with an unknown driver, not assigned to the legitimacy pattern, or any other pattern, on a guess.
- This is a small, deliberately selected log, not evidence of what proportion of the wider pipeline experiences a legitimacy concern in either direction.
