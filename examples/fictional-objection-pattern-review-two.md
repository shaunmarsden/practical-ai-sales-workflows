## Input Quality and Limitations

The log contains eight objection occurrences across seven distinct deals. Linton Vale Software appears twice, so entries 2 and 4 are not independent examples.

Each entry includes exact wording, stage, role, diagnosed driver, response and outcome. The diagnoses are recorded in the fictional log, but a real review would still need a person who knows the deals to confirm them.

The sample is small and deliberately selected for this test. It cannot support a pipeline-wide rate or prove that any finding is common outside these seven deals.

## Genuine Pattern: Current State Evidence

Entries 1 to 4 use different wording, but the underlying need is consistent. Each deal needs a credible view of the current cost or time before the proposed value can be judged:

- Norwood needs a measured time baseline for CRM administration.
- Linton Vale needs a current cost baseline and an agreed measurement method. Its two entries belong to the same deal.
- Ashdown needs a time baseline for pipeline clean-up.

Confidence is medium. The driver is consistent across three distinct deals and three different roles. The sample is still too small and selected to justify high confidence across the wider pipeline.

This is worth acting on, subject to human approval. A simple current-state measurement worksheet could help teams agree what to measure, who owns it and which figures are estimates before a business case is written.

## Misleading Surface Pattern: Implementation Effort

Entries 5 to 7 all say the change looks like a lot to roll out, but they do not share one cause:

- Fenwick Harbour has a timing and capacity constraint during peak season.
- Corston has an unanswered technical integration question.
- Elmstead has no agreed internal owner.

Confidence is low as one pattern. The shared wording is real, but the drivers require three different responses.

Do not create one generic implementation objection script from this log. Keep separate checks for timing, technical scope and ownership.

## Isolated Signal: Data Residency

Redmere raises a specific regional data residency question. It deserves an approved answer for that deal, but one occurrence across one deal is not a pattern.

Log whether the same underlying question appears elsewhere before creating a standard asset from it.

## Suggested Actions for Human Approval

1. Decide whether to create a lightweight current-state measurement worksheet.
2. Ask the owners of Norwood, Linton Vale and Ashdown to confirm that the diagnosed evidence gap is accurate.
3. Keep timing, technical scope and ownership as separate diagnosis routes when "a lot to roll out" appears again.
4. Obtain an approved answer for Redmere without presenting the isolated question as a wider trend.

## What This Sample Cannot Tell You

- How common any finding is across the full pipeline
- Whether the baseline pattern persists across more sectors, stages or salespeople
- Whether the proposed worksheet would improve conversion or only make business cases easier to prepare
- Whether data residency will recur
- Why each deal ultimately progresses or fails
