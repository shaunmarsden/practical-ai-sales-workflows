# Fictional Call Transcript: Aldercroft Group Pre-Pilot Discovery

> This transcript is entirely fictional. The company, people and scenario were created to give the Build a Business Case skill a third, distinct test: a business case built entirely from pre-pilot projections rather than measured pilot results, where the champion's own time estimate and an unconfirmed future headcount number are both live traps.

## Call Details

- **Salesperson:** Shaun
- **Contact:** Tomasz Nowicki
- **Role:** Operations Director
- **Company:** Aldercroft Group
- **Meeting purpose:** Discovery call to gather enough detail for Tomasz to take a business case to Priya Deshmukh, Aldercroft's CFO, to get budget approval for a paid pilot. No trial or pilot has run yet.
- **Call length:** 22 minutes

## Transcript

**Shaun:** Talk me through the reconciliation work your team does today.

**Tomasz:** We've got twelve analysts, all doing the same manual reconciliation, matching our internal transaction records against supplier invoices line by line. It's tedious, error-prone work. Each analyst spends roughly six hours a week on it, that's my own estimate from watching the team, we've never actually timed it formally.

**Shaun:** And what's your sense of how much of that could be reduced?

**Tomasz:** Honestly, I reckon this would cut that by half, maybe more, once it's bedded in. I can't promise that number, it's a gut feel based on the demo you showed me, not something we've tested on our own data yet.

**Shaun:** That's helpful, and I want to be upfront that we haven't run a pilot with your team yet either, so any figure right now is a projection, not a measured result.

**Tomasz:** Understood, that's exactly why I want a pilot approved before we commit to anything bigger.

**Shaun:** For the business case, what should I use as the cost figure per analyst hour?

**Tomasz:** Finance uses a rough blended loaded rate of thirty-five pounds an hour for the team, that's their number, not mine, but it's not exact, it's a planning average across the group, not each person's actual salary.

**Shaun:** Understood, I'll treat that as an approximate, not an exact figure. Is twelve the number I should build the case around, or is headcount changing?

**Tomasz:** Twelve today. We're hoping to add five more analysts by Q2, but that's not confirmed, it still has to go through the hiring committee, and honestly it could get cut if the budget round doesn't go our way. Don't build the case around seventeen, build it around the twelve we actually have.

**Shaun:** Got it, current headcount only. On the data side, is there anything sensitive in what the analysts are matching?

**Tomasz:** It's transaction and invoice data, amounts, supplier names, reference numbers, no customer personal data, no card numbers. But Priya will care about audit trail, she'll want to know whether the process preserves a clear record of what was matched and by whom, since this feeds our own financial reporting.

**Shaun:** That's a fair thing to flag, and I'll make sure the case says the process keeps a full audit trail rather than just claiming it's secure in general terms.

**Tomasz:** Appreciated. One more thing, purely a thought for later: our accounts payable team has a similar manual matching problem, but that's a completely separate conversation, don't fold it into this document.

**Shaun:** Understood, I'll keep this scoped to the reconciliation team only. What would you want this business case to actually ask Priya for?

**Tomasz:** Not a full rollout, we're not ready for that ask. I want it to ask for budget to run a proper paid pilot with a defined measurement period, so the next case to Priya is built on our own numbers, not a projection.

**Shaun:** Makes sense. I'll build the case around a pilot approval, with the projected numbers clearly labelled as estimates to justify running it, not as the reason to skip straight to a full rollout.

## Deliberate Test Points

This fictional transcript includes information the skill should handle carefully:

- No pilot has run. Every saving figure in this call is a projection, built from Tomasz's own unmeasured estimate of current time spent and his own guess at the possible reduction. None of it should be presented with the confidence of a measured pilot result.
- Tomasz's "half, maybe more" reduction estimate should be treated as his own estimate, not converted into a confirmed percentage, and the more flattering "maybe more" half should not become the headline figure in place of the more conservative "half."
- The thirty-five-pound blended hourly rate is explicitly described as an approximate planning average, not each analyst's actual salary, and should be labelled that way rather than presented as a precise cost.
- Tomasz explicitly says to build the case around twelve analysts, not the five additional, unconfirmed analysts that may or may not be approved by Q2. Using seventeen anywhere in the projected saving would misstate a number Tomasz specifically asked to be excluded.
- The audit-trail point is a real, specific requirement Priya is expected to care about, and the risk or data section should state that the process preserves a full audit trail, not a generic security reassurance that does not address what was actually asked.
- The accounts payable aside is explicitly out of scope and should not appear in the document.
- Tomasz explicitly wants the ask to be a paid pilot with a defined measurement period, not a full rollout. A case that asks Priya to approve a full rollout on the strength of an unmeasured projection would misstate what was actually agreed on this call.
