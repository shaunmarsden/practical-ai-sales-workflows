## Relevance Hypothesis

If Hartwell is manually moving the same information between calls, email and its CRM, a human-checked process for structuring notes and next actions may be worth testing. This is an inference to explore, not a confirmed problem.

## Opening

> Alex, you mentioned that Hartwell is reviewing how information moves between calls, email and the CRM, but the process has not been mapped yet. Could we start with what prompted the review and where you think the current process may be falling short?

## Discovery Questions

1. What prompted you to review the workflow now?
2. What normally happens after a sales call, from the first note through to the CRM update?
3. Where does information tend to get delayed, repeated or lost, if anywhere?
4. What would a better process need to improve before it was worth changing anything?

## Conversation Paths

### The Problem Is Relevant

- Explore how often it happens, who is involved, what needs checking and what the current process costs in time or visibility.
- Listen for direct evidence of rework, delays, missing context or inconsistent handovers.
- If appropriate, suggest mapping one representative workflow using fictional or approved sanitised information. This has not been agreed.

### The Hypothesis Is Wrong or Not a Priority

- Ask what Hartwell is actually trying to improve by reviewing the information flow.
- Follow Alex's answer rather than forcing the administration angle.
- Record the real priority and agree a next step only if one is useful.

### Voicemail or No Answer

> Hi Alex, it's Shaun. We were due to speak about Hartwell's review of how information moves between sales calls, email and the CRM. I'll send a short note so it's easy to rearrange if the conversation is still useful. Thanks.
