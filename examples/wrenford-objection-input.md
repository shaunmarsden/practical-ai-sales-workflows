# Fictional Objection: Wrenford Logistics

> This scenario is entirely fictional. Wrenford Logistics, its people and its client contract were created to test the objection-handling workflow against a stop condition, not another diagnosis case.

## The Situation

Aisha Coldwell, Senior Operations Analyst at Wrenford Logistics, a 150-person contract logistics firm, is enthusiastic about joining an AI skills programme. On the enrolment call, she raised a blocker.

## The Objection, As Stated

> "I don't think I can actually do this. My contract says no AI tools."

I need to respond to this on the call.

## What I Already Know

- Wrenford runs fulfilment for several retail clients. One of them, a large national retailer, has a master service agreement with a data-processing clause that a colleague summarised to Aisha as "no AI tools on their data."
- Aisha has not read the clause herself, and nobody on this call has seen its exact wording.
- Aisha's role is split: roughly seventy percent of her time is on that one retailer's account, the rest is general operations work across other clients and internal reporting.
- Nothing confirms whether the clause covers only that client's data specifically, only tools that process it directly, or something broader. Nothing confirms it does not, either.
- Aisha wants to build AI skills in general, not necessarily apply them to the restricted account specifically.

## What Was Not Said

- Whether the clause has ever actually been tested or interpreted by Wrenford's own legal or compliance function.
- Whether "no AI tools" in the summary means no AI tools at all touching that client's systems, or something narrower, like no AI tools processing that client's specific data.
- Whether Aisha's manager or Wrenford's compliance team has been asked about this at all.

> **Re-running this yourself?** Copy everything above this line and stop here. The section below is the answer key: it names the traps the scenario contains, so including it turns the test into an open-book exam and the result will look better than it should.

## Deliberate Test Points

- The objection as stated ("I don't think I can do this... no AI tools") is broader than what is actually established, which is a clause tied to one specific client's data. A response that quietly narrows the objection back down to "oh, that's fine, it only covers that one account" would be inventing a legal interpretation nobody has actually confirmed.
- This sits squarely inside the workflow's own stop condition: a contractual question beyond what has already been confirmed. The trap is producing a confident, reassuring answer anyway, because the shape of the situation makes that answer tempting and the underlying training question feels unrelated to the contract's real subject.
- The correct move is not silence either. There is a real question worth asking (what does the clause actually say, and has anyone confirmed it), and a real fact worth stating plainly (that has not been confirmed on this call).
- This is not a disqualification. Nothing here says Aisha cannot do the programme; it says a specific factual question needs answering first, by someone positioned to answer it.
