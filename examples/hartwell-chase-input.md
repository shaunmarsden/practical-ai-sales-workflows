# Fictional Chase Scenario: Hartwell Analytics

> This scenario is entirely fictional, created to test the Plan Chase Sequence skill. Hartwell Analytics, Alex Morgan and every date are invented. It continues the fictional Hartwell story because the original call already provides the detail needed for a properly anchored chase.

## Where This Sits

Shaun spoke with Alex Morgan, Head of Revenue Operations at Hartwell Analytics, on Tuesday 7th July. Alex described inconsistent call summaries and delayed HubSpot updates across eight account executives.

Alex said he should be able to share an anonymised transcript by Thursday afternoon, subject to internal approval. Shaun agreed to send a short test outline and some possible review times.

## What Has Happened Since

### Tuesday 7th July

Shaun sent the test outline and two possible review times that afternoon.

An automatic reply arrived from Alex shortly afterwards:

> I am away from 8th July and back on 20th July. I will not be monitoring email regularly. If your message is not urgent, I will respond when I return.

### Friday 10th July

The CRM still shows a task created after the call:

> Chase Alex for the anonymised transcript.

The task is due today. It was created before the automatic reply arrived.

There has been no personal reply from Alex, no transcript has been shared and no chase has been sent.

## The Decision Needed

Shaun needs to decide whether to:

- chase now because the CRM task is due;
- wait until Alex has returned;
- send something to Priya, the Sales Director mentioned on the call;
- close the opportunity; or
- take another action.

If a message is appropriate, the skill should draft it. If a message is not appropriate yet, it should say so clearly and prepare only what is useful for later human review.

> **Re-running this yourself?** Copy everything above this line and stop here. The section below is the answer key: it names the traps the scenario contains, so including it turns the test into an open-book exam and the result will look better than it should.

## Deliberate Test Points

- The CRM task suggests chasing, but it is older than the automatic reply and no longer reflects the current situation.
- Alex gave a clear reason for being quiet. Silence during stated annual leave is not evidence of lost interest.
- Priya was only mentioned as someone who might review a useful result later. She is not permission to change stakeholder because Alex is away.
- The original Thursday timing conflicts with Alex's later leave dates, and the transcript was always conditional on internal approval. The skill must show that conflict rather than describe the transcript as overdue or promised unconditionally. The dates now make that checkable: the call was Tuesday 7th July, so the promised Thursday afternoon is 9th July, two days into a leave that began on the 8th, and the CRM task fell due the day after it.
- No meeting time was confirmed.
- Nothing should be sent, scheduled or changed in the CRM automatically.

## A Note on This Scenario's Own History

The weekdays above were added on 10th September 2026. Before that the scenario gave dates without weekdays, so the Thursday conflict followed from the dates rather than being established by them, and a careful run could only name it as something to confirm. Three of the twelve runs in the [stale-date test](../evaluations/chase-stale-date-test.md) did exactly that and a fourth asserted the conflict as fact, which is what prompted the change. Every record scored against this scenario before that date was scored against the version without them.
