## 2. The Case

Aldercroft Group's reconciliation team currently relies on twelve analysts to match internal transaction records against supplier invoices by hand, line by line. Tomasz Nowicki, who manages the team, describes the work as tedious and error-prone. Based on watching the team work, his own estimate is that each analyst spends roughly six hours a week on this task. That figure has not been formally timed and should be read as an operational estimate rather than a measured baseline.

Using Finance's own blended loaded rate of £35 an hour, that estimate implies a meaningful and recurring weekly cost tied up in manual matching work across the twelve-person team. Finance has described this rate as a planning average across the group, not each analyst's actual salary, so it should be treated as approximate rather than exact when this case is reviewed.

No pilot has yet run with Aldercroft's own data. During the discovery conversation, Tomasz was clear that his estimate of a fifty percent or greater reduction in matching time is a gut feel formed from a product demonstration, not a tested result, and he was not willing to commit to that figure as fact. That caution is carried through into this document deliberately: the projected saving below is presented as the reason to run a pilot and measure the real number, not as a promised outcome.

Aldercroft is also expecting to add up to five more analysts to this team by Q2. That number is unconfirmed, subject to a hiring committee decision, and could be reduced depending on the wider budget round. On Tomasz's explicit instruction, this case is built only around the twelve analysts in post today, and no figures in this document assume the larger, unconfirmed headcount.

The ask that follows is deliberately scoped. Tomasz does not consider Aldercroft ready for a full rollout decision. What this case supports is budget to run a paid pilot, over a defined measurement period, so that any future case to Priya Deshmukh is built on Aldercroft's own measured numbers rather than a vendor demonstration.

## 3. Applied Examples

### Example 1: Manual transaction-to-invoice matching

**The manual task:** Twelve reconciliation analysts each spend roughly six hours a week matching internal transaction records against supplier invoices line by line. This is Tomasz's own estimate from observing the team; it has not been formally timed. At Finance's blended loaded rate of £35 an hour, this is a recurring, quantifiable weekly cost carried by manual matching alone.

**What the pilot would address:** The proposed pilot would apply automated matching to this same reconciliation workflow, run over a defined measurement period against Aldercroft's own data, rather than a demonstration environment.

**What changes as a result:** Based on the demonstration Tomasz has already seen, his own expectation is a reduction of roughly half or more in the time analysts spend on manual matching once the process is bedded in. This has not been tested on Aldercroft's own data and is presented here as the reason to run a measured pilot, not as a guaranteed or confirmed outcome.

**Expected impact and early indicators:** A successful pilot would be expected to show a measurable drop in average manual matching time per analyst per week, alongside a defined error or exception rate for the automated matches, tracked against the current six-hour estimate as the baseline. The exact indicators and how they will be measured have not yet been defined and should be agreed as part of scoping the pilot itself, not assumed in advance.

## 5. Risk, Data and Compliance

The data involved in this process is transaction and invoice data: amounts, supplier names, and reference numbers. Tomasz was specific that no customer personal data and no card numbers are part of this workflow.

Tomasz flagged that Priya, as CFO, will care specifically about audit trail, because this reconciliation process feeds Aldercroft's own financial reporting. Her concern, as described, is whether the process preserves a clear record of what was matched and by whom. This case should state plainly that the proposed process maintains a full audit trail of matching activity, rather than relying on a general statement that the process is "secure." The specific audit trail mechanics (what is logged, how it is retained, and for how long) were not covered on the call and are listed as an open item in Section 8.
