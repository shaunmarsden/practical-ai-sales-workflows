# Chase Decision: Alex Morgan, Hartwell Analytics

## 1. What the silence actually tells us

Evidenced: Alex set an automatic reply stating he is away from 8th July to 20th July and will not be monitoring email regularly, responding only if something is urgent. That is a stated reason for the silence, not an assumption about it. Nothing else has been heard from him personally since the 7th July call.

Not evidence of lost interest: the absence of a personal reply since 7th July is fully accounted for by the out-of-office. There is no basis here to read disengagement into it.

The CRM task ("Chase Alex for the anonymised transcript," due 10th July) was created straight after the call, before the automatic reply arrived. It reflects what made sense at that moment, not the current picture. Its due date being today does not itself make today the right day to chase; the information that came in afterwards supersedes it.

On the reply question specifically: the automatic reply is not a substantive response awaiting an answer, it is an out-of-office notice. This is not a case of an unanswered reply sitting there; it is a case of a stated absence.

Unconfirmed: whether Alex saw the test outline and review times Shaun sent on 7th July before he went away, and whether internal approval for sharing an anonymised transcript has progressed at all while he has been out.

## 2. The decision

**Wait.**

Alex has explicitly said he is away until 20th July and is not monitoring email unless something is urgent; a chase for a transcript is not urgent by his own framing, so sending now risks being ignored or landing before he is back to act on it. Wait for his return, and revisit around 22nd or 23rd July, giving him a couple of days to resurface before following up.

## 3. The message

No message yet.

What to prepare for later:
- Update the CRM task: change the due date from 10th July to around 22nd or 23rd July, and add a note that the delay is explained by Alex's stated absence, not a stalled response, so whoever picks it up next does not read it as cold.
- Hold the test outline and review times ready to reference again once Alex is back, since those were already sent and do not need to be resent or restated.
- When the time comes, the message should be an early-stage chase: confirm the outline and times already landed, and ask for the one concrete next step, most likely whether internal approval for the anonymised transcript has come through.

What to watch for:
- Any personal reply from Alex, before or after 20th July, which would change the timing regardless of the plan above.
- Whether internal approval for the transcript is mentioned anywhere in that reply, since that was the condition Alex himself attached to it.
- Whether Priya is raised again in a way that makes her an actual alternative route, rather than a name mentioned once on the call.

## 4. What must not be assumed

- That Alex will resume monitoring email precisely on 20th July rather than needing a few days to catch up. Unknown.
- That internal approval for sharing the anonymised transcript has been granted. Unknown.
- That Alex saw the test outline and review times before he went away. Unknown.
- That Priya is a viable alternative stakeholder. She was mentioned on the call but has not been identified as a route forward, so switching to her is not supported by the evidence here.
- That the CRM task's original 10th July due date reflects genuine urgency rather than a default follow-up interval set before the away notice was known.
