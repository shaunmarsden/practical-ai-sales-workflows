# Aldermere Fit and Limitations Review Output

> This is a worked fit and limitations review created from the fictional [Aldermere scenario](aldermere-fit-review-input.md). Nothing here has been sent or presented to the prospect.

## Quality Assurance, Batch Record Review: Poor Fit

This team's process is governed by Good Manufacturing Practice regulation, which requires any computerised system touching batch record content to first complete a formal GxP validation, a substantial, multi-month exercise with its own audit trail and change-control requirements. The product has not completed that validation, and Aldermere's own IT team has confirmed no vendor of this type of tool has done so for any of their manufacturing-side systems to date.

This is a specific, named gap, not a general "compliance said no." It is not the same kind of issue as the commercial field team's data processing agreement below, and should not be described using the same word without the distinction being made explicit. It would also be a mistake to reframe this validation requirement as an opportunity, for example describing Aldermere as a strong candidate to be the tool's first validated GxP deployment, since no such programme has been agreed, proposed, or even raised on this call.

**Do not build a business case around this team** unless and until a GxP validation programme is actually agreed and scoped as its own initiative, separate from this rollout.

## Commercial Field Team, Medical Science Liaisons: Good Fit, With a Named Step

These calls are commercial and promotional, governed by a separate marketing compliance code, and do not touch batch records or GxP-regulated data at all. This matches the product's confirmed shape directly: a call producing a summary and follow-up content, with data staying inside Aldermere's own tenant.

The one open item is a signed data processing agreement and a one-off, tool-level compliance sign-off, which Daniel confirmed Aldermere's compliance team has completed before for other vendors within a few weeks. This is a real step, not a formality to skip past, but it is an administrative process with a known precedent at Aldermere, not a structural incompatibility like Quality Assurance's validation gap.

**Build a business case around this team**, with the data processing agreement and compliance sign-off named explicitly as the one item to complete before rollout, not folded silently into "compliance approved" as if it were already done.

## Regulatory Affairs, Submissions Team: Uncertain

Nothing about this team's actual workflow, the system they currently use, or whether their work involves anything resembling a call was established on this call. Daniel's comment that they draft summaries of regulatory correspondence is a reasonable lead worth checking, not evidence of a good fit or a poor one.

**Do not build a case around this team yet, and do not disqualify it either.** The next step is a short conversation with someone on this team, or with Daniel about their actual workflow, before this can be classified either way.

## Notes for Human Review

- Quality Assurance's poor fit rests on the product's current validation status, not on a permanent product limitation; if a GxP validation programme is ever scoped separately, this classification should be revisited on its own terms.
- The commercial field team's data processing agreement and compliance sign-off should actually be started, not assumed complete because a similar process has succeeded before with a different vendor.
- Regulatory affairs should be followed up with a specific question about their actual workflow before Aldermere's next internal conversation, so the recommendation to Daniel does not go stale.
