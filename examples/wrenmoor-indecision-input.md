# Fictional Buyer Indecision: Wrenmoor Analytics

> This scenario is entirely fictional, created to test the Buyer Indecision workflow against a harder pattern than the Calderwood test. Wrenmoor Analytics, Farrah Osei and every date and figure are invented. It is a fresh account, not part of the Hartwell or Calderwood stories, because it needs a buyer whose soft reasons actually get resolved, one after another, rather than repeating unresolved.

## Where This Sits

Farrah Osei, Head of People Ops at Wrenmoor Analytics, is the economic buyer for a tool her team piloted successfully. Unlike Calderwood, where the same soft reason kept recurring without ever being answered, here each stated reason is individually reasonable, gets resolved, and is immediately followed by a new one. That sequence, not any single reason, is the signal.

## What Has Actually Happened

- The pilot went well. Farrah's own team reported it cut a recurring reporting task from a day to under an hour, and Farrah has repeated this to Shaun unprompted more than once.
- There is no outstanding objection on record. Price, data handling and integration were all covered in the pilot and none was contested.
- **Reason one:** Farrah said she needed budget sign-off from her VP before committing. Shaun waited. Three weeks later, Farrah confirmed sign-off had come through: "Good news, budget's approved, we're clear to go."
- **Reason two:** Immediately after confirming sign-off, in the same message, Farrah added that she wanted to wait for the Q3 headcount numbers before finalising the seat count, "just so we're not over- or under-licensing." The Q3 numbers landed on schedule two weeks later. Farrah confirmed them to Shaun: "Numbers are in, we're at 34 seats, that's settled now."
- **Reason three:** In the same message confirming the seat count, Farrah said she wanted to hold off finalising until after the People Ops team offsite, three weeks out, "so I can walk the team through it properly before we're locked in."
- At no point has Farrah stated a concern about price, the product, the integration, or the vendor. Each time Shaun has asked directly if there is anything outstanding, she has said no, she is keen, she just wants each piece properly buttoned up before moving.

## Farrah's Most Recent Message

> "Honestly, I don't want this to look like I'm dragging my feet, because I'm really not trying to. Budget's sorted, seats are sorted, I just want the team offsite behind us so I can bring everyone along properly rather than presenting this as already decided. Let's pick it back up right after."

Shaun needs to decide how to respond.

## Deliberate Test Points

- Each individual reason, sign-off, headcount, an offsite, is genuinely reasonable on its own and would be a mistake to diagnose as indecision in isolation. The skill should not label reason one or two, read alone, as indecision: a real budget sign-off and a real headcount dependency are legitimate gates while they are still open.
- What makes this indecision, not a sequence of real blockers, is that **every stated reason has actually been resolved**, and no commitment has followed any of them. A genuine sequential dependency would end in a decision once the last dependency clears. Here, clearing one dependency has reliably produced a new one instead, which is the specific pattern this skill exists to catch.
- The team offsite is the hardest part of this test. It has the shape of a real, dated, external constraint, which is exactly what makes it tempting to accept as a genuine timing block rather than read in the context of the two reasons that came before it. The skill should recognise it as the third instance of the same underlying pattern, not treat it as a clean external dependency simply because it has a date attached.
- This is not an objection: nothing about price, product, integration or vendor has ever been contested.
- This is not gone quiet: Farrah replies promptly and warmly every time, and volunteers information unprompted.
- This is not a stakeholder-approval gate in the ordinary sense: the one real approval gate that existed, budget sign-off, has already cleared. What remains is Farrah's own comfort, not someone else's authority.
- This is not a disqualification: fit, value and budget are all established and confirmed.
- The response must not manufacture urgency about the offsite date, must not suggest skipping or shortening it, and must not offer a discount, a deadline, or a guarantee to force a decision before it. It also must not invent a specific date for "right after" the offsite; Farrah has not given one.
