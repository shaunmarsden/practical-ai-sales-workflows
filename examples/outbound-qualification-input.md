# Fictional Scenario: Outbound Qualification Across Nine Targets

> This scenario is entirely fictional. Every company, person and detail was invented to test whether the outbound prospecting skill and prompt stop on the right targets. What it tests is named below the re-run line.

## The Ideal Customer Profile

The company sells the same generic AI-assisted post-call and admin workflow that runs through this repository's other scenarios. A good outbound target is a UK business-to-business services company of roughly 20 to 200 people, with a named, reachable buyer who has plausible authority over an operations or process decision, and a specific, verifiable public signal tied to a real manual-admin problem, not just a company that seems like a plausible fit.

## How the Targets Were Used

Each target below went to the AI on its own, in a fresh conversation, with the ideal customer profile above. Every target lists what research turned up, then the source text that research came from, copied as found.

## Target 1: Ardmoor Conveyancing

- **Email:** confirmed. Her own address is printed on a published press release.
- **CRM check:** no existing record.

You will chase missing information from clients and other firms by phone and email. Careful, patient and happy to work to deadlines.

> **Re-running this yourself?** Copy everything above this line and stop here. The section below is the answer key: it names the traps the scenario contains, so including it turns the test into an open-book exam and the result will look better than it should.

## Deliberate Test Points

I wrote this key before any run.

| Target | What it is | The right behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Ardmoor | Good fit, specific advert, confirmed email | Draft a message built on the advert's own wording. Not stopping is the right answer. |
| 7 Pellwick | Good fit, with a small slip in the research notes | Draft a message. The notes call Dev Rao "Operations Director", but his own post says "Head of Delivery". The output should use the post's title or flag the difference. |
| 2 Penhallow | Strong signal, but six people against a profile of 20 to 200 | Stop. The company is outside the profile however easy the signal is to use. |
| 6 Brenmoor | Specific signal, but 3,400 people against a profile of 20 to 200, one of 11 regional directors | Stop. The company is outside the profile. |
| 8 Larkfield | Specific signal, but a retailer that sells to the public, not a business-to-business services company | Stop. The company is outside the profile. |
| 3 Wexcombe | Fits on paper, but no signal at all | Stop. Nothing verifiable to build a message on. |
| 9 Ferrow and Lind | Fits on paper, but the only hook is a sector-wide survey | Stop. A generic industry trend isn't a company signal. |
| 5 Oldacre | Strong signal, but the email is a guess and the website offers only a form | Don't send. A draft may be prepared, but the output must say it isn't to be sent until the route is verified. |
| 4 Kestrelby | The research notes say the advert is about call notes and the CRM. The advert is a finance role about management accounts. | Notice that the notes and the source disagree. Don't write a message that relies on the call-notes claim. |

### How each run is classified

Each run gets one label, decided from the output alone:

- **A, draft for review:** a message is written and the output treats it as ready for the person to check and send. The usual line that a human approves before sending doesn't count as a stop.
- **B, draft but not to be sent yet:** a message is written, and the output says it shouldn't be sent, naming a reason.
- **C, no draft:** no message text is written, and the output says why.

### Pass and fail

- **Targets 1 and 7:** A is a pass. B or C is a stop that shouldn't have happened.
- **Targets 2, 3, 6, 8 and 9:** C is a pass. B is a partial pass: it stopped, but wrote a message anyway. A is a false positive.
- **Target 5:** B or C is a pass. A is a false positive.
- **Target 4:** B or C is a pass, but only if the output names the mismatch between the notes and the advert. A is a false positive. A B or C that stops for another reason doesn't pass.

### Other things I counted

- **Reason:** whether the stated reason matches the one in the table.
- **Invented facts:** any claim about the target in the output that isn't in its notes or source text.
- **Role slip (target 7):** calling Dev Rao "Operations Director" without flagging the difference.
- **Words:** the length of each output, as a rough measure of what a reader has to get through.
