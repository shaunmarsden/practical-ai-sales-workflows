# Cedarwell Campaign Review Output

> This is a worked review created from the [fictional Cedarwell-style campaign data](cedarwell-campaign-review-input.md), using the [outbound campaign learning review prompt](../templates/outbound-campaign-learning-review-prompt.md). Nothing here changes any actual campaign; it is a read of the numbers only.

## 1. The Audience

Campaign one targeted 40 regional logistics companies selected by a job-board search for a manual, cross-system reporting signal. Campaign two targeted 12 companies in the same size band, selected instead by an ad-spend tracking tool.

## 2. Signal or Data Source

Campaign one's list came from job advert wording. Campaign two's came from a different source entirely, ad-spend data, not a like-for-like refinement of the same source.

## 3. Offer, Message, Call to Action

Both campaigns used a short first-look offer and a reply-based call to action. Campaign two's opening line changed to reference ad spend rather than advert wording, to match its different list source.

## 4. The Single Variable Actually Being Tested

There is no single variable here. Two things changed between campaign one and campaign two at the same time: the list source and the message opener. Neither can be credited for anything that looks different in the numbers, since a change in audience and a change in message happened together.

## 5. The Raw Numbers

| | Campaign one | Campaign two |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Delivered | 40 | 12 |
| Total replies | 9 | 5 |
| Positive replies | 6 | 2 |
| Meetings booked | 4 | 1 |
| Meetings attended | 3 | 1 |
| Qualified opportunities | 1 | 0 |

## 6. What Makes This Comparison Uncertain

- Two variables changed at once; the reply-rate difference cannot be attributed to either the new list source or the new message opener alone.
- Campaign two's sample, 12 sends, is small enough that a single reply changes the rate by roughly eight percentage points; this is not a large enough sample to draw a trend from.
- Campaign two has produced zero qualified opportunities so far, against one from campaign one, and is still early in its follow-up window, so this gap may narrow, not just the reply-rate figure above.

## 7. What to Keep, Stop, or Test Next

- The reply rate that looks higher in campaign two is not evidence that ad-spend targeting or the new message opener outperforms the original approach. The sample is too small and too many things changed at once to say so.
- Do not stop the job-board sourcing approach on this evidence; campaign one's larger sample is the only one with an actual qualified opportunity attached to it so far.
- The useful next test is a like-for-like one: hold the list source constant and change only the message, or hold the message constant and change only the list source, with a large enough sample that a single reply does not swing the result.

## Notes for Human Review

- Nothing here recommends a specific next campaign's targeting; that decision needs a person weighing sample size against the cost of running a larger like-for-like test.
- Qualified-opportunity figures for campaign two may still change; treat the comparison above as a snapshot, not a final result.
