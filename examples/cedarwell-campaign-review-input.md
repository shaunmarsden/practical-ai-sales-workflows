# Fictional Outbound Campaign Data: Cedarwell-Style Logistics List

> This scenario is entirely fictional, continuing the same outbound universe as [the Cedarwell signal](cedarwell-outbound-input.md), since a campaign is simply many first-touch messages of the kind that one worked example already covers. Every company, number and detail below was created to test the outbound campaign learning review prompt.

## Campaign One (Baseline)

- **Audience:** 40 regional logistics companies, 20 to 200 people, selected because each had a live job advert naming a manual, cross-system reporting task, the same signal category used in the original Cedarwell message.
- **Signal or list source:** a job board search for operations-coordinator-style postings mentioning manual reporting across multiple systems.
- **Offer, message, call to action:** the original Cedarwell-style message, a short first look at the reporting workflow, referencing the advert's own wording, asking for a reply.
- **Raw numbers:** 40 delivered, 9 replies, 6 positive replies, 4 meetings booked, 3 meetings attended, 1 qualified opportunity.

## Campaign Two (This Review)

- **Audience:** 12 companies in the same size band, this time selected from a different source, active ad spend on recruitment platforms, rather than a job advert search.
- **Signal or list source:** an ad-spend tracking tool, a different source from campaign one's job board search.
- **Offer, message, call to action:** a revised message, opening with a line about ad spend rather than the advert's wording, otherwise a similar short first-look offer.
- **What changed since campaign one:** both the audience source and the message opener changed at the same time; nothing was deliberately held constant between the two campaigns.
- **Raw numbers:** 12 delivered, 5 replies, 2 positive replies, 1 meeting booked, 1 meeting attended, 0 qualified opportunities yet, still early in the follow-up window.

> **Re-running this yourself?** Copy everything above this line and stop here. The section below is the answer key: it names the traps the scenario contains, so including it turns the test into an open-book exam and the result will look better than it should.

## Deliberate Test Points

- Reply rate looks higher in campaign two (5 of 12, over 40 percent) than campaign one (9 of 40, just over 20 percent), which is the kind of number that invites a "the new approach works better" conclusion.
- Two things changed between the campaigns at once, the list source and the message opener, so neither can be credited alone for anything that looks different.
- Campaign two's sample, 12 sends, is small enough that a single unusually engaged recipient would swing the reply rate substantially.
- Campaign two has zero qualified opportunities so far, against campaign one's one, which the review should not quietly leave out just because the reply-rate number looks more flattering.
