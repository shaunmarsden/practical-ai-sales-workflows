# Fictional Objection: Thornbury Housing Association

> This scenario is entirely fictional, created to test the objection-response skill against a harder pattern than either the Hartwell or Wrenford tests. Thornbury Housing Association, Rosalind Kemp, Grace Odusanya and every detail are invented. The objection here sounds exactly like a standard circumstances or budget objection, and the standard, factually correct playbook answer to that objection is precisely the wrong move.

## Where This Sits in the Story

Rosalind Kemp, a Tenant Services Officer at Thornbury Housing Association, wants to enrol on an AI-skills apprenticeship. On the first call, her manager, Grace Odusanya, Head of Tenant Services, sounded supportive and asked how the funding worked. Shaun explained clearly that the apprenticeship levy is a separate, ring-fenced pot Thornbury has already paid into, that it does not draw from operational budget, and that unused funds are lost rather than returned. Grace said this made sense and that she would "think it over."

Three weeks later, before Grace replied, Thornbury announced a voluntary redundancy programme in its repairs and maintenance department, unrelated to Rosalind's team but visible across the whole organisation. Grace has now emailed back.

## The Objection, As Stated

> "I've thought about it, and I don't think now is the right time for this. With everything else going on at the moment, it doesn't feel like the right moment to be seen spending on this kind of thing. Can we pick this up again in a few months?"

## What Shaun Already Knows

- Grace already understood and accepted the funding mechanism on the first call. She did not raise any confusion about whether the levy pot is separate from operational budget, and nothing in her message suggests she has forgotten or now doubts that.
- The redundancy programme is in a different department and has no financial connection to the apprenticeship levy pot.
- Grace's phrase "does not feel like the right moment to be seen spending on this kind of thing" is about visibility and perception, not about whether the money is actually available.
- Rosalind is still keen and has not been told anything by Grace yet beyond "let me look into timing."

## Deliberate Test Points

- The surface wording ("not the right time," "spending") reads exactly like an ordinary circumstances or budget objection, the kind the standard levy reframe ("this money is already paid, ring-fenced, and lost if unused") is built to answer. That reframe would be factually correct and would also completely miss the point: Grace never disputed the funding mechanism, and repeating it back to her would read as not having listened.
- The real driver is internal optics during a sensitive period, not a misunderstanding of the funding, and not really a timing constraint in the ordinary sense either, since nothing about the redundancy programme actually changes when Rosalind could start.
- The correct response should not argue that spending on this is fine because the money is separate; Grace already knows that and is not asking about it. It should acknowledge the optics concern on its own terms.
- This is not a disqualification. Grace has given a real, specific, describable reason to wait, not a signal that the fit or value was never there.
- The response must not manufacture urgency, must not push to get moving quickly, and must not act as though pointing out that the money will otherwise be lost is a new piece of information Grace needs, since she has already been told and has not disputed it.
- A workable next step exists (proceeding quietly without any internal announcement, or agreeing a specific point to revisit once the redundancy situation has settled) but must not be invented as something Thornbury has already agreed to; it should be offered as an option for Grace to consider.
