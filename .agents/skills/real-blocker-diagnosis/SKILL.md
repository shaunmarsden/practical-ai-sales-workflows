# Real Blocker Diagnosis

> Landed here directly rather than clicking through from a guide? This file is the instruction sheet an AI assistant follows, not written for a first read start to finish. [What is a sales AI skill?](../../../guides/what-is-a-sales-ai-skill.md) has the plain-English version.

You do not need to install anything to try this once. The lines between the dashes at the very top are just this file's label; leave them in. On GitHub, copy this using the **Raw** button near the top of the page rather than selecting the rendered text, so the tables and links below paste in cleanly. Send the whole file as your first message in any AI chat tool, then follow it with your actual inputs.

The person speaking on a call is not always the person who decides, and the reason someone gives is not always the reason actually driving the outcome. This skill checks both, using only what was actually said and who actually attended, never inventing a hidden motive with nothing behind it.

## Gather the Inputs

- Who was expected on the call, and who actually attended
- What each attendee's role or title is, where known
- What each attendee actually said, especially anything raised as a concern or objection
- Anything already confirmed about who holds budget or sign-off authority

## Check Who Actually Attended

Compare the expected attendee list with who actually joined. An unplanned or last-minute attendee is worth naming explicitly, not quietly folded into "the prospect team." Note it even when nothing about the meeting content yet suggests it will matter.

## Check Role Against Stated Concern

For each attendee, compare their role or title against what they actually raised. A title suggests an area of responsibility; it does not confirm what a specific person is actually thinking about today. When a stated concern sits outside what the title would predict, name the mismatch plainly rather than treating the title as the more reliable signal.

## Separate the Stated Reason From the Checkable Fact

State the concern exactly as given, then note, separately, what it would actually take to resolve it. An unplanned join or a title mismatch is a reason to ask a further question, not a reason to assume you already know the real answer. Never invent what the underlying motive actually is; flag that one is worth checking for.

## Confirm Who Actually Decides

Do not treat enthusiasm, or being the point of contact, as confirmation of budget or sign-off authority. If no one on the call has explicitly confirmed holding that authority, say so, and name who else may need to be identified and briefed before this moves further.

## Apply the Guardrails

- Never assert a hidden motive the evidence does not actually support; flag the mismatch, do not invent the explanation for it
- Never treat the most talkative or most senior-sounding attendee as the decision-maker without an explicit confirmation
- Never treat a stated objection as fully resolved just because a plausible-sounding answer was given, if the underlying authority or motive question was never actually tested
- Never contact anyone not already on the thread to test any of this; recommend the next question, do not send it

## Stop When the Task Is Unsafe

Do not produce a full diagnosis when:

- Nothing in the call notes suggests any mismatch between role and stated concern, and no unplanned attendee joined; there is nothing here to diagnose
- The only support for a hidden motive is speculation with no attendee statement or behaviour actually behind it

## Require Human Review

This surfaces a mismatch worth checking, not a verified explanation. The seller decides whether and how to raise it, and who to actually contact next; nothing here contacts anyone or treats a diagnosis as confirmed.
