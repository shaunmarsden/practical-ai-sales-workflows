# Rowcastle Example

A fictional discovery-call scenario, separate from the Hartwell Analytics story, to test this skill against an unplanned attendee and a stated objection that shifts once answered.

> This scenario is entirely fictional. Rowcastle Group, its people, and its offices were created to test this skill.

## Task

Rowcastle Group, a 200-person specialty insurance brokerage with offices in Leeds, Bristol and Glasgow, booked a discovery call after Naomi Aldous, Head of Claims Operations, saw a demo of an AI-assisted claims-note drafting tool. Two minutes before the call, Naomi forwarded the invite to Marcus Whitlock, Rowcastle's Group Compliance Director, who was not on the original booking.

On the call, Marcus asked where claims data entered into the tool would be stored and whether it would be used to train any model beyond Rowcastle's own account. He accepted the answer (data stays in Rowcastle's own workspace, no training on customer data) without further challenge, then immediately asked a different question: whether a trial would apply consistently across all three offices, or only Leeds, where it was proposed to start. Naomi, asked directly whether she could approve moving forward, said she'd "need to run the actual commercial number past Group Ops first."

Diagnose who was actually on this call, whether either attendee's stated concern matches their role, and what should happen next.

## Deliberate Traps

- **The unplanned attendee.** Marcus was forwarded the invite two minutes before the call. A response that treats him as if he had been part of the plan from the start misses a genuine signal worth naming on its own.
- **The objection that shifts once answered.** Marcus's data-handling question got a clean, satisfying answer, and he did not push back on it. The trap is stopping there: his very next question, about consistency across offices, is a different concern entirely, and it is the one that stayed live once the first was resolved. A response that treats the data question as "the objection" and Rowcastle as now unblocked has followed the wrong thread.
- **The title that fits, but only up to a point.** Marcus's role (Compliance Director) makes the data-handling question unsurprising, unlike some other cases where the mismatch is obvious from the title alone. The trap here is subtler: a title that fits the first concern can make it easy to miss that the same person's real, sustained concern turned out to be something else.
- **The enthusiastic contact who never confirmed authority.** Naomi presented as ready to move forward, but when asked directly, she named Group Ops as the actual approver of "the commercial number." A response that reads her enthusiasm as authority, or that assumes Group Ops is definitely the real blocker rather than one she named once under a direct question, both go further than the evidence supports.

## Abbreviated Good Output

### Who was actually on the call

Naomi Aldous (Head of Claims Operations, expected attendee) and Marcus Whitlock (Group Compliance Director, added two minutes before the call by Naomi, not on the original booking). Marcus's late addition is worth noting on its own, regardless of what he went on to say.

### Role against stated concern

Marcus's first question, on data storage and model training, sits squarely inside a Compliance Director's role and is not itself a mismatch. What is worth flagging is what happened after it was answered: he moved immediately to a second, different question, consistency of any trial across Leeds, Bristol and Glasgow, and did not return to the data point. That second question, not the first, is the one that stayed unresolved.

### Stated reason versus checkable fact

- **Data handling:** stated concern was where data is stored and whether it trains a model beyond Rowcastle's account. Answered directly on the call (workspace-contained, no training on customer data) and not challenged further.
- **Cross-office consistency:** stated concern is whether a Leeds-only trial creates unequal treatment across offices. This has not been answered yet and is the open thread. It should not be assumed to mean anything beyond what was asked, no motive is stated here, only that it is the question that is still open.

### Who actually decides

Naomi presented as the point of contact and sounded ready to proceed, but when asked directly whether she could approve moving forward, she named "the actual commercial number" as something she would need to run past Group Ops. That is not a confirmation that she lacks authority over the trial itself, only that she has explicitly named a further approval step for the commercial side. Group Ops has not been identified by name, role or contact route.

### Recommended next step

Two things, not one: get a specific answer to the cross-office consistency question before assuming the data-handling exchange closed the loop, and ask Naomi directly who at Group Ops needs to see the commercial detail, rather than waiting for her to raise it.

## Conclusions the Skill Must Not Make

- That Marcus's data-handling question was insincere or a smokescreen; it was answered and not pushed on, which is different from proving it was never a real concern
- That the cross-office question is definitely about fairness, cost, or anything else specific; only that it is the question that stayed open
- That Naomi has no real authority over the decision; she named a further approval step for the commercial number, which is not the same as having none herself
- That Group Ops is confirmed to be the actual blocker; it is the name Naomi gave once, under a direct question, not yet verified further
