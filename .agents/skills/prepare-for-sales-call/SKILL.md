# Prepare for a Sales Call

> Landed here directly rather than clicking through from a guide? This file is the instruction sheet an AI assistant follows, not written for a first read start to finish. [What is a sales AI skill?](../../../guides/what-is-a-sales-ai-skill.md) has the plain-English version.

You do not need to install anything to try this once: copy this whole file, paste it as your first message in any AI chat tool, then follow it with your actual inputs.

Build a short card a salesperson can scan during a live conversation. Organise evidence, expose gaps and prepare useful questions without manufacturing a reason the prospect should buy.

## Gather the Inputs

- Contact name, role and company
- Meeting purpose and the salesperson's good outcome for the call
- Current CRM record, relevant email history and previous interactions
- Approved public company information, if it is genuinely useful
- What the salesperson can help with, stated without private pricing or unsupported claims

Ask for the minimum missing information if the contact, company or meeting purpose is unknown. A card without those three things is guesswork.

## Build a Source Ledger

List the useful sources, when each was checked and what it actually supports before writing the card. Prefer:

1. Direct customer statements and agreed actions
2. Current CRM records and email history
3. Previous meeting notes
4. Official public company information
5. AI interpretation

Show conflicts instead of quietly choosing whichever source creates the strongest sales angle.

## Separate Evidence From the Working Theory

Use these sections:

- **Confirmed context:** directly supported by the supplied sources
- **Public background:** verified public information, useful as context but not proof of an internal problem
- **Assumptions to test:** plausible ideas that still need discovery
- **Unknowns:** missing information that matters
- **Relevance hypothesis:** one short, testable connection between the context and the problem the salesperson may be able to solve

Never turn a job advert, company announcement or contact title into proof of pain, urgency, budget or buying authority.

## Prepare the Conversation

Write:

- one natural opening anchored to the meeting purpose;
- four short discovery questions;
- a path if the hypothesis is relevant;
- a path if it is wrong or not a priority; and
- a short voicemail or no-answer message.

Keep it brief enough to scan during the call. Follow the section order in the [Pre Call Card](../../../templates/pre-call-card.md) and the [Writing Style and Formatting guide](../../../guides/writing-style-and-formatting.md). Do not add a second risks or sources section after the human check.

## Apply the Guardrails

- Do not invent company initiatives, internal problems, technologies, budgets, authority, urgency or commitments.
- Do not use public information as evidence of a private operational problem.
- Do not hide missing or conflicting information inside polished prose.
- Do not include sensitive or unnecessary personal information.
- Do not turn a preparation card into a rigid call script.
- Do not treat a proposed next step as agreed before the conversation happens.
- Keep reader-facing titles free of hyphens and do not use em dashes.
- Do not use bold labels followed by colons inside bullet lists.

## Stop When the Task Is Unsafe

Stop and ask for clarification when:

- the named contact, company or meeting purpose cannot be confirmed;
- critical sources conflict and the conflict would materially change the call;
- the request depends on private company context the user has not confirmed they are allowed to use; or
- the only available relevance angle is an unsupported public inference.

## Require Human Review

The salesperson checks every source, removes anything inappropriate and decides which questions or conversation path to use. This skill does not contact the prospect, book a meeting or change the CRM.
