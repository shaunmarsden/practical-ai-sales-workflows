# Output Template: Opportunity Handover

> Instructions: the AI must output the handover using this exact Markdown structure. Use "Unknown" where a material point is missing rather than forcing an empty section to look complete.

## 1. 30 Second Brief

No more than four bullets: the opportunity, the current position, the most important caution, and the immediate next action.

## 2. Current Position

The newest reliable position, stated plainly, with the date or relative timing of the evidence it rests on.

## 3. Recipient-Specific Context

Why this handover is happening now, who it is for, and anything specific to what the receiving person needs to pick this up.

## 4. People and Confirmed Roles

For each person: name, confirmed role, involvement, and anything still uncertain. State plainly if a contact or owner has changed since the earlier evidence.

## 5. Customer Expectations and Confirmed Commitments

What the customer has actually said they want or agreed to, kept separate from anything only discussed or suggested.

## 6. Desired Outcomes and Possible Measures

The customer's intended outcome, and any measure or baseline that is actually supported by the evidence. Leave this section out entirely, rather than filling it with a guess, if nothing is supported.

## 7. Confirmed Evidence

Only information directly supported by the supplied sources, each with a short source label.

## 8. Estimates, Inferences, Unknowns and Conflicts

Four separate groups. Preserve uncertainty and show any material conflict between sources rather than resolving it.

## 9. Actions and Ownership

A table with Owner, Action, Timing, Status and Evidence. Every row needs exactly one named internal owner, never a compound name, "the customer," or "unassigned." If the party who must actually act is external or not yet confirmed, the Owner column still names the internal person chasing it, and the Action or Evidence column can note who externally needs to respond. Drop an action once it is stale or superseded rather than carrying it forward out of habit. Do not treat a suggestion or conditional statement as an agreement.

## 10. Risks, Cautions and Non-Standard Commitments

Anything that could cause the receiving person to overstate the opportunity, miss an action, misuse information, or treat an unusual commitment as standard.

## 11. Recommended First Focus

One recommended focus for the receiving person, supported by the evidence, stating what must be checked first and who currently owns it.

## 12. Useful Sources

The source names or links the receiving person should keep. Do not claim to have opened or verified a link unless its contents were actually supplied.

## 13. Questions to Answer Before Ownership Is Accepted

The most important questions the receiving person should ask before accepting ownership of this opportunity or account.
